Getting into the answer

Beyond RV is already being fetched and cited by AI. What it is not doing is giving the citation anything to quote. This is the plan to fix that, and then to widen it.

The problem has changed since the first look

A second, better-researched AI answer cited Beyond RV twice, with links. That is the retrieved path working. The trouble is what it found there.

You were ranked fourth of five, and described in adjectives while everyone else got numbers.

The model quoted Wedgetail at 517 kg and $54,199 with a 12-year structural warranty; Trayon at 390–410 kg, family-owned since 1994, 10-year structural off-road warranty; Active Campers at 530–550 kg.

Beyond RV got prices — $72,000 to $77,800 — and then “heavier class”. No weight. No warranty term. No year the company started.

This is not a model overlooking you. It read your pages, took what was quotable, and where it found a number it printed the number. Where it found “Confirm with Beyond RV” — which is what the Advent pages say under dry weight — it wrote an adjective. In a comparison table an adjective beside four numbers reads as a weakness whether or not it is one.

The same happened with the warranty. The warranty page sets out what is covered in careful detail and states no term. Competitors publishing “12 years” and “10 years” got those figures printed as a reason to choose them.

That is the whole gap, and it is unusually cheap to close.

The model also handed you your positioning, unprompted. It concluded that Wedgetail and Trayon maximise off-road mobility, while the Advent maximises living comfort — and recommended it for an older couple touring for months, wanting instant access without raising a roof or unfolding canvas.

That is a better description of the difference than most marketing copy manages, and it is worth adopting rather than arguing with. Trying to beat Trayon at 390 kg is a fight with the laws of physics. Owning the hard-wall alternative is a fight you have already won on the product.

What the research says earns a citation

Three findings that decide where the ninety days go.

Fact density is the lever a small company can actually pull. Work from Princeton, Georgia Tech and the Allen Institute found that adding citations to primary sources, attributed statistics and quotes from named people lifted how often AI engines quoted a page by 30 to 40% — and that it lifted lower-ranked sites most.

You will not out-rank Trayon on domain authority this year. You can out-cite them on specifics.

Princeton / Georgia Tech / Allen Institute for AI, via 2026 GEO research summaries

Reddit and YouTube are where AI reads. Across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot and Google AI Overviews, Reddit is the single most-cited social source, at 53.6–62.8% of social citations. Reddit and YouTube together account for 78.2% of all social citations. Wikipedia and Reddit alone drive over a quarter of ChatGPT's US citations.

Platform matters: Google AI Overviews and Perplexity cite YouTube heavily — 36.6% and 38.7% of YouTube citations respectively — while ChatGPT cites it far less, at 9.5%.

llms.txt does not influence citations. Google likened it to the old keywords meta tag. You already have one, correctly written. That is the right amount of effort to spend on it: no more.

The plan

Four phases. They overlap, but they are ordered by dependency: earlier ones create the material the later ones need.

Week 1

Publish the numbers you are being marked down for

The cheapest and fastest item on this list. Four facts, on every camper page, as plain text and in the product schema so they are machine-readable:

  1. Dry weight in kilograms, per model. This is the single figure the comparison turned on.
  2. Warranty term in years. Whatever it genuinely is. The page already describes the cover well; it needs the number.
  3. Construction — composite panel, hard wall — in the words a buyer would search.
  4. Where it is built, honestly. Imported shell, completed and certified in Mutdapilly. Said plainly it is a fact; left vague it looks like something being avoided.

Add the year the business started, on the About page, for the same reason Trayon's “since 1994” got quoted.

One caution. If “Confirm with Beyond RV” is deliberate — because units vary by fitout, or because nobody wants to be held to a figure — then say that, with a range and what moves it. A published range beats an invitation to phone. What cannot continue is being the only entry in the table without a number.

Build the 52 pages nobody else can write

One page per vehicle, generated from the data already in the catalogue. Slide-on campers for the Ford Ranger, and the same for HiLux, Triton, Amarok, D-Max, BT-50, Navara, the Unimogs, the trucks.

Each page answers, with real numbers: what this ute's GVM and kerb mass are, what payload is left, which Beyond RV campers fit within it, which do not and by how much, and what a GVM upgrade changes. Cite the manufacturer document each figure came from — that is the fact density the research describes, and you already hold the sources.

This is the fast path. “What is the best slide-on camper for a Ranger with 900 kg of payload?” cannot be answered from a model's memory; the figures are too specific. It has to fetch a page, and yours would be the only one that has them.

  • Generate from the existing 168 variants and 52 models — the data exists, this is a template and a build.
  • Put the answer in the first paragraph, before any sales copy.
  • Mark up each page as a question and answer, as the calculator page already is.
  • Link each to the calculator so a reader can change the numbers.

Be useful on Reddit, where the models read

Reddit is the most-cited source in AI answers, because it is where real people argue about real purchases. Australian touring subreddits carry the exact question you can answer better than anyone: will this fit, and is it legal loaded.

The rule that decides whether this works: answer the question fully in the comment. Give the payload figure, the maths and the verdict. Link the calculator only as the place to check their own numbers. A comment that answers gets quoted; a comment that says “great question, see our website” gets downvoted and, increasingly, ignored by the models too.

  • Use a named account that says who you are. Undisclosed promotion gets banned, and a ban is public and permanent.
  • Read each subreddit's self-promotion rule before the first comment, not after.
  • Twenty minutes a day, genuinely helping. This is the one part of the plan that cannot be delegated to a contractor.

Write the comparison from your own side

The model placed you fourth on a list sorted by weight and off-road ability. On a list sorted by living in it for three months in the rain, the order changes, and nobody has written that list.

Write it. Hard wall against canvas: setup time, weather, warmth, noise, security, what it costs in kilograms. Name Wedgetail and Trayon, quote their real weights, and concede the point honestly — they are lighter, and for the Canning Stock Route that matters more. Then make the case where yours wins.

A comparison that concedes gets cited. One that does not, does not — and readers can tell the difference as quickly as models can.

Collect the reviews that do not exist

There is no rating signal anywhere, because there is nothing to mark up. For a best in Australia question that is the strongest missing input, and customers give it free if asked at the right moment.

Ask at handover, while they are standing next to the camper and pleased with it. Not by email a fortnight later. Then mark the reviews up on the site so they are machine-readable as well as human-readable.

Ten genuine Google reviews changes what a search shows. Fifty changes what an AI believes about you.

Get onto YouTube and into the awards

YouTube is the second most-cited social source, and Google's own AI Overviews lean on it hardest. A review by an Australian touring channel is watched, quoted, transcribed and cited — one good one outperforms everything else on this list.

Lend a unit for a fortnight. Ask for an honest review, not a favourable one; an obviously sponsored puff piece is worth less to you than a review that names a real drawback and still recommends the camper.

In parallel, enter the awards. Wedgetail is named in the search results specifically because Camper Trailer Australia put it in Camper Trailer of the Year. Award coverage is written by editors, indexed for years, and quoted long afterwards.

What not to spend money on

Anything sold as “getting you into AI answers”. You are already in them. What you are missing is quotable facts about your own campers, and no agency can supply those — only a set of scales and a decision about the warranty.

More technical AI-readiness work. The crawler permissions, the llms.txt, the schema and the static rendering are all in place and verified. Google has said llms.txt does not influence citations. Anyone quoting for this is quoting for work already done.

Anything promising fast results on “best camper” questions. That answer comes from what models already learnt, which cannot be edited. The questions you can win quickly are the specific ones — a ute, a payload, a fit — because those must be looked up.

Buying reviews, or posting on Reddit without saying who you are. Both are detectable, both get punished, and a public ban is a permanent third-party mention of exactly the wrong kind.

Volume for its own sake. Thirty-six pages is few, but the answer is not three hundred thin ones. It is fifty-two pages that each hold a number nobody else has.
